# Existing operational bird occurrence and migration forecast products (state as of 24 October 2026)

Scope: products that predict or visualise where and when birds will be, with the Netherlands first, then Belgium, Germany, UK, Denmark, France, and US products as method references. Purpose: judge which approaches a hobby developer (Vogelradar, vogel-radar.nl, rare bird sightings scraped from waarneming.nl) could copy for a rare bird occurrence heatmap for the Netherlands and neighbouring countries.

Method note: findings below were taken from pages fetched directly on 24 October 2026 unless stated otherwise. Where a page could not be fetched and the fact comes only from a search-result summary, this is flagged with "(search summary)". Two open-access PDFs (De Koning 2025 in Ecological Informatics; Nature news feature of 16 October 2025) were downloaded and read in full via text extraction. Pages that returned 403/404 or certificate errors are listed in the Gaps of each section.

## Key question 1: The Sensing Clues crane migration forecast (Kraanvogelradar)

### Takeaway
The "Kraanvogelradar" (https://analytical.sensingclues.org/cranes/, also reachable via kraanvogelradar.nl) is a real-time, species-specific "digital twin" built by WUR researcher Koen de Koning with the Sensing Clues Foundation under the EU Horizon Europe project Nature FIRST. It does not predict where cranes will appear from scratch: it takes live "flying over" crane observations from the waarneming.nl/observation.org API, propagates each observed flock forward with a flight model (speed and heading, corrected for KNMI HARMONIE wind forecasts, parameters trained on GPS tracks plus ERA5 reanalysis), and renders a grid-based probability layer of where flocks will pass in the next tens of minutes to a few hours. It is an R Shiny app that re-runs every one to five minutes, cost roughly 15,000 to 20,000 euro and about a year to build, and its model code (minus API credentials) is deposited at 4TU.ResearchData under a published paper.

Inputs
- Observations: cranes reported as "flying over" via waarneming.nl and observation.org; the paper counts "a quarter million migration observations between 2009 and 2024" (footnote link: https://observation.org/species/19/statistics/?activity!=PRESENT). Filters "select the observations only of cranes reported as 'flying over' (sometimes including direction e.g. 'flying south'), within the study area and seen not more than thirty minutes ago". [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- The public page says the model uses sightings by birders in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany via Waarneming.nl, "current weather data", "over 20,000 historic sightings" and knowledge of the species' biology and ecology. [Sensing Clues crane radar page](https://sensingclues.org/craneradar)
- Wind: "The crane radar only uses wind-related data as environmental data which has a good predictability within the forecast period of the crane radar." Wind forecasts come from the KNMI data platform API (footnote: https://dataplatform.knmi.nl/group/weather-forecast), "to obtain hourly weather forecast data"; "Weather forecasts on the other hand update every six hours, and therefore only four API calls per day are required". [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- The Sensing Clues release post names "wind data from the KNMI HARMONIE model" and says the connection to it was improved. [Sensing Clues news: Latest Crane Migration Radar is here](https://www.sensingclues.org/news/crane-migration-radar)
- Training data: "These algorithms are parametrised using historical GPS tracks and weather reanalysis data (Hersbach et al., 2023)" (Hersbach et al. 2023 is the ERA5 hourly pressure-level dataset in the reference list). [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- Nature says the radar "uses migration data (sourced from Movebank, an online animal-tracking database), real-time sightings by birdwatchers and factors such as wind speed and direction of flight to estimate where the birds will be within the next four hours". [Nature 646:763-765](https://media.nature.com/original/magazine-assets/d41586-025-03314-y/d41586-025-03314-y.pdf)
- The 2022 WUR English news item says De Koning's analysis of GPS-tracked cranes found a strong relationship between departure and wind speed, wind direction, precipitation, cloud cover and temperature, with north-east wind and calmer, cooler conditions acting as departure signals (search summary). [WUR news EN: Follow crane migration live](https://www.wur.nl/en/news/follow-crane-migration-live-crane-radar)

Model and method
- "The crane radar's flight algorithms are grounded in long-established models and theories on bird migration and flight optimisation (Alerstam and Hedenström, 1998; Liechti, 1995; Newton, 2010; Pennycuick, 1978)"; details are in De Koning et al. 2024. [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- Data assimilation step: "Whenever the location of a newly observed flock falls within the confidence limits of any flock forecasted by the digital twin (coloured clouds in Fig. 2), the model links the new observation to the most likely flock in the digital twin. This allows re-parameterisation of the predicted speed and direction of the cranes". [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- Uncertainty is shown as "a raster-based probability map that shows the likelihood of cranes flying over a certain area", adopted after user feedback. [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- The 2024 companion paper (De Koning, Nilsson, Månsson, Ovaskainen, Kranstauber, Arp, Schakel; Ecological Modelling 498:110884; CC BY 4.0) presents "three species-specific individual-based models" operating at "kilometres, 15 min-hours", using "GPS-tracked location, flying direction and speed, and/or wind data to forecast migration speed and direction", cross-validated on GPS tracks. Findings: recorded flight speed and direction improve forecasts under 2 h; wind at flight altitude improves forecasts over the whole range, mostly for speed; direction changes less because cranes compensate for drift; spring is harder than autumn; "flight speed is the most difficult parameter to forecast" while direction is most critical for applications such as bird-strike prevention. [JYX record of the 2024 paper](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/97690); [DOI 10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2024.110884](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2024.110884)
- WUR summarised the same paper: with headwind cranes fly faster, with tailwind slower; with crosswind they change heading; in autumn they compensate more than in spring. [WUR nieuws: vernieuwde kraanvogelradar, 28 Oct 2024](https://www.wur.nl/nl/nieuws/volg-de-kraanvogeltrek-nog-beter-met-de-vernieuwde-kraanvogelradar)
- Oct 2024 version: "a grid with colour codes showing the likelihood" that a previously spotted group passes a location "in the next ten minutes"; the previous version "showed a spot predicting the path from wind direction and speed"; "A new algorithm, which includes, among other markers, the birds' velocity, makes the predicted path much more accurate." [Resource, 24 Oct 2024](https://www.resource-online.nl/index.php/2024/10/24/crane-radar-improves-probability-calculation/?lang=en)
- Sensing Clues: "Koen developed a new method for calculating the probability of crane migration paths using a grid-based approach", giving "a more accurate visualization at any zoom level" and fixing limits of the previous heat map on busy days; the release moved the radar to Sensing Clues' "new analytical platform"; hour-ahead forecasts "similar to Buienradar" were mentioned as future work. The post is dated "Oct 1" without a year. [Sensing Clues news: Latest Crane Migration Radar is here](https://www.sensingclues.org/news/crane-migration-radar)
- 2022 version as described by BNNVARA Vroege Vogels: a "buienradar" for cranes; from the flight direction and approximate speed of a group reported on waarneming.nl it calculates where the group will be after some minutes or hours, shown as a red cloud (search summary). [BNNVARA Vroege Vogels](https://www.bnnvara.nl/vroegevogels/artikelen/trekkende-kraanvogels-volgen-via-een-app)

Technology stack and deployment
- "The entire model is developed using shiny ... an R-package used to creative interactive apps in an R environment"; "At initialisation, authorisation tokens are obtained from the waarneming.nl/observation.org API and the KNMI data platform API upon first deployment of the app on the server. Once running, a continuous loop is triggered using the reactiveTimer() function". Figure 3 describes a separate server-side process "based on python code" outside the Shiny app. Deployment challenges listed: transferring dependencies, keeping R packages up to date, securing API tokens, robustness (tryCatch), and "Container-based approaches such as Docker ... are helpful tools". [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- API access: "The APIs of waarneming.nl and observation.org for the crane radar could be accessed through personal connections with one of the founders of the platforms. Once accessed there is clear documentation on how to use the API, including example scripts of API calls. The API of the KNMI open data platform ... is easier to access and requires only a free-of-charge subscription". [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- Data-quality issues named: observers reporting their own location instead of the birds', duplicates of the same flock by different observers, and the time lag between seeing and uploading ("many birdwatchers do not immediately report their observations in the field but later back at home, making their observations already obsolete"). [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)

### Inferences
- The forecast horizon evolved: a 4-hour flock projection (paper, Nature), a 10-minute grid probability (Oct 2024 Resource article), and a "Nu / Komend uur" (now / next hour) toggle on the current page. The current public product most likely shows a one-hour-ahead probability grid, with longer flock tracks drawn as lines; this is inferred from the page markup, not confirmed by live data.
- The planned 48-hour forecast (announced March 2025 for autumn 2025) is not visibly documented as delivered; the Feb 2026 newsletter does not mention it. Status unknown.
- The SensingClues fork of harmonie-grib (updated 18 Oct 2026) is very likely the wind-ingestion component of the radar, given the paper's reference to a separate Python process on the server and to KNMI HARMONIE wind; this is an inference.
- The approach is a nowcasting data-assimilation design (observations + physics-like propagation + wind), not a statistical occurrence model. It works because cranes are large, diurnal, flocking, reported with direction, and follow narrow corridors. For rarities (stationary, singular, unpredictable arrival) the propagation part does not transfer; the reusable parts are: the ingestion loop, the grid probability rendering with plain-language legend, user location alerting, 1 to 5 minute refresh, and the explicit treatment of reporting lag and location imprecision.
- The hardest dependency for a copycat is API access to waarneming.nl/observation.org, which the radar obtained via personal contacts with a platform founder, not via a public developer programme.

## Key question 2: BirdCast and European radar-based migration products (KNMI/UvA FlySafe, CROW, aloft, bioRad, Trektellen, offshore wind forecasts)

### Takeaway
BirdCast (US) forecasts nocturnal migration intensity from a machine-learning model trained on 23 years of weather-radar bird detections and NARR reanalysis weather, updated every 6 hours, with 24 to 72 hour forecasts explaining 68 to 72 percent of variance; it is a graphics product with citation terms, not a documented open data API. Europe has no public equivalent forecast map, but the ingredients are open: KNMI publishes FlySafe bird-density composites and 5-minute real-time products under CC BY 4.0, Belgium's CROW shows live radar migration for the Benelux from an open vpts repository, and the aloft/BALTRAD/UVA vpts datasets cover 141 to 151 European radars back to 2008. A UvA 48-hour North Sea migration forecast exists for offshore wind curtailment but is not public.

### Cited Findings

BirdCast (US, method reference)
- birdcast.info now redirects (301) to birdcast.org (verified by curl). Forecast maps "show predicted nocturnal migration approximately three hours after local sunset and are updated every 6 hours"; "the models predict the migration intensity for birds in active flight; these are not predictions of the numbers of birds on the ground". [BirdCast Forecast Maps](https://birdcast.org/migration-tools/migration-forecast-maps/)
- "The model underlying the migration forecasts was trained on historical NCEP North American Regional Reanalysis (NARR) data and 23 years of bird movements in the atmosphere as detected by the U.S. weather surveillance radar network. By learning associations between weather variables and migration, we can use the model to predict a bird migration intensity given a particular weather forecast." Maps also outline expected precipitation in grayscale. Forecasts are produced by Purdue University, University of Illinois and the Cornell Lab; the required model citation is Van Doren and Horton 2018, Science 361:1115-1118, doi 10.1126/science.aat7526. [BirdCast Forecast Maps](https://birdcast.org/migration-tools/migration-forecast-maps/)
- Van Doren and Horton (preprint abstract): 23 years of spring observations; models "explained up to 81% of variation in migration intensity" across the US at 0-3000 m; forecasts 24-72 hours ahead "explained 68-72% of variation"; migration often exceeds 200 million birds per night and 500 million at peak. [bioRxiv 293092](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/293092v1)
- Live maps: near real-time from the US weather surveillance radar network between local sunset and sunrise; intensity as migration traffic rate (MTR) in birds/km/hour; orange arrows show flight direction; contiguous US only; radars in mountainous areas may show false "no migration". [BirdCast Live Maps](https://birdcast.org/migration-tools/live-migration-maps/)
- Four products: Live Maps, Forecast Maps, "Your Local Dashboard" (county and state), Migration Alerts ("Email alerts available in 216 U.S. cities"). [BirdCast Migration Tools](https://birdcast.org/migration-tools/)
- Team and institutions: Adriaan Dokter (project leader, Cornell Lab; author of the bioRad R package), Daniel Sheldon (UMass; leads the NSF BirdFlow project "which uses eBird data to infer and predict bird movements"), Kyle Horton (Purdue), Benjamin Van Doren (UIUC); support from NSF and Leon Levy Foundation. [BirdCast About](https://birdcast.org/about/)
- The dashboard lets users go back to past nights; location search is limited to the contiguous US (search summary). [All About Birds: How to use the BirdCast Dashboard](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/news/?p=34689)
- A separate study reports a 2.9 x 2.9 km radar-driven forecast model for part of the Mississippi flyway (search summary; page not fetched). [NSF PAR 10092591](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10092591)

Netherlands: FlySafe, KNMI Data Platform, UvA
- UvA (17 June 2026): FlySafe is an Air Force system that maps and predicts bird migration, operated by KNMI, running "almost fifteen years", "recently modernized with better algorithms and interactive maps", using weather radars in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany; UvA models "link bird migration observations from radar data to weather conditions"; Shamoun-Baranes: "In this way you can predict when large-scale bird migration will start and at what altitude the birds will be flying"; "Through the KNMI Data Platform, bird profiles and migration maps are publicly available for research and other applications"; researchers "are working on predictive models at the European scale". [UvA news, 17 June 2026](https://www.uva.nl/en/content/news/news/2026/06/mapping-busy-bird-migration-airspace-together-with-the-air-force-and-the-knmi.html)
- KNMI dataset radar_flysafe_nl_composite v1.0: gridded composite of vertically integrated bird density over the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany from all operational weather radars in those countries, with BIRDTAM values, NetCDF, licence CC BY 4.0, updated continually, coverage from 2026-01-01; bioRad estimates a vertical bird profile that is applied to reflectivity within 80 km of each radar, so precipitation can be counted as birds; BIRDTAM 9 or higher is assumed non-ornithological (search summary of the dataset page). [KNMI Data Platform dataset](https://dataplatform.knmi.nl/dataset/radar-flysafe-nl-composite-1-0)
- A separate KNMI listing describes 5-minute real-time bird density and BIRDTAM values for the Netherlands, available via Open Data API, notification service and WMS (search summary). [KNMI Data Platform, tag Birds](https://dataplatform.knmi.nl/dataset/?tags=Birds)
- KNMI project page "Estimating bird migration using weather radar" exists. [KNMI research project](https://www.knmi.nl/research/observations-data-technology/projects/estimating-bird-migration-using-weather-radar)
- Offshore wind: "the University of Amsterdam developed a bird migration forecast model to predict bird migration at the North Sea 48 hours in advance. This forecast model has an autumn and a spring module, both delivered in 2022." The model predicts MTR "in the lower 300 meters above sea level and is trained on weather data from the ERA5 reanalysis model", with ECMWF forecast data as operational input; an expert team of seven ornithologists judges whether curtailment is necessary when a threshold is passed; validation against Luchterduinen radars found "the MTRs produced by the bird migration forecast model were lower than the MTRs calculated using the radars". Report by Waardenburg Ecology for Rijkswaterstaat, nr 23-130, 14 November 2023. ## Key question 6: Products or write-ups that try to predict where a rare or vagrant bird will appear next

### Takeaway
No operational rarity or vagrancy forecast product exists for the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany or the UK as far as could be found; the only operational-style systems in the literature are species-specific (the Dutch crane radar, and a 2024 Japanese "rare bird forecast" for Ross's Gull that converts numerical weather prediction into 1 to 5 day daily observation probabilities with up to 95 percent predicted probability on validated days). UK practice consists of human weather-chart reading (BTO blog, BirdGuides articles) and the published view that forecasting rarities is "a mug's game", with the scientific caveat (Gilroy and Lees) that observer coverage, not weather, largely determines where vagrants are found.

### Cited Findings
- "Rare bird forecast: A combined approach using a long-term dataset of an Arctic seabird and a numerical weather prediction model" (Ecology and Evolution 14(6), e11388, June 2024): aims "to develop real-time forecast systems for the vagrancy/migration events of a rare avian species using a numerical weather prediction model", using long-term Ross's Gull monitoring in northern Japan; Method A links sightings to pressure patterns over the Sea of Okhotsk with self-organizing maps, Method B is a regression on local environmental factors; both run on the operational global NWP model to forecast daily observation probabilities 1 to 5 days ahead from November to February; Method A gave 0 to 85.7 percent, Method B 0 to 95.5 percent; northerly wind strength and advancing season explained sightings; a 2-year follow-up validated both, especially when both forecast high probabilities (search summary). [PMC11199128](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11199128/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/d203017182e84c2d85b1661ed7476f60)
- Brooks (2024, Movement Ecology) used eBird records of land passerines over the Pacific to model offshore vagrancy and found vagrancy "more common in the fall and positively associated with stronger tail winds in the spring" (search summary; full text behind Springer login). [DOAJ record, Movement Ecology Sep 2024](https://doaj.org/article/42bf398ec67e43eda6e85e0a77305ad2)
- Gilroy and Lees, "Vagrancy theories: are autumn vagrants really reverse migrants?": the "reverse migration shadow" has been used "to identify regions in which autumn vagrancy of a given species may occur, and to predict potential future vagrants to Britain"; their finding is that "vagrancy during autumn migration occurs in all directions, and we contend that it is the pattern of observer coverage which determines the number of vagrants discovered" (search summary). [UEA research portal](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/vagrancy-theories-are-autumn-vagrants-really-reverse-migrants/)
- Lees and Gilroy, Vagrancy in Birds (Helm, 2021) is a global assessment of how and why birds stray out of range (search summary). [UEA eprints](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/85379)
- BirdGuides article "Predicting Nearctic passerines in autumn" describes using weather charts to anticipate North American passerine arrivals in Britain, referencing Elkins' 1983 Weather and Bird Behaviour and Lees and Gilroy's 2009 chapter (search summary; page returned 403). [BirdGuides article](https://www.birdguides.com/articles/migration/predicting-nearctic-passerines-in-autumn/)
- Bozó et al. (2016, Ornis Hungarica) compared autumn timing of nine Siberian warblers in the Russian Far East (2011-2014) with their occurrence in Europe and concluded most individuals of several species reach Europe because of Siberian cyclones, with reverse migration, weather and dispersal also playing a role for Yellow-browed Warbler (search summary). [Ornis Hungarica 2016](https://reference-global.com/article/10.1515/orhu-2016-0009)
- A British Birds letter on the October 1982 influx proposed that "up-to-date weather data from Siberia could possibly give advance indication of large-scale movements towards Europe" (search summary). [British Birds letters](https://www.britishbirds.co.uk/journal/article/letters-84)
- BirdGuides (Dominic Mitchell) on British "next first" predictions: a 1993 bookmaker's form card tipped 57 possible firsts and the next actual first was not among the favourites; "forecasting rarities is a mug's game" (search summary). [BirdGuides: Dominic Mitchell, first impressions](https://www.birdguides.com/articles/dominic-mitchell-first-impressions)
- A US (Maine) birding blog pairs wind forecasts with expected migrant pushes but notes there is no single pattern that strongly suggests any particular vagrant (search summary; posts from 2014). [Maine Birding Field Notes](https://mebirdingfieldnotes.blog/page/45/)
- Dutch anecdotes: a Desert Wheatear at Schiphol "probably came over because of an easterly wind"; persistent easterlies make it "quite possible that more birds are carried further west than usual" (search summaries). [BNNVARA: Woestijntapuit](https://bnnvara.nl/community/fotos/vogels/woestijntapuit/300672); [BNNVARA: bergheggenmus](https://www.bnnvara.nl/vroegevogels/artikelen/tussen-de-trekvogels-zat-de-bergheggenmus)
- BirdCast's team page states that the NSF BirdFlow project "uses eBird data to infer and predict bird movements". [BirdCast About](https://birdcast.org/about/)

### Inferences
- The Ross's Gull paper is the closest published blueprint for a rarity forecast: (a) a long historical record of a species' occurrences at known sites, (b) a classifier of synoptic weather patterns (SOM on pressure fields) and a regression on local wind and season, (c) daily probabilities driven by an operational NWP forecast, (d) validation on later seasons. Each step is feasible with waarneming.nl history for the Netherlands plus ERA5 (training) and ECMWF open data or KNMI HARMONIE (forecast), species by species or per species group (for example Siberian passerines in October under easterlies, Nearctic waders under Atlantic lows).
- The scientific literature (Gilroy and Lees) warns that an occurrence model trained on waarneming.nl records will learn observer density (coastal hotspots, Texel, Maasvlakte) more than bird arrival; any heatmap should be presented as "chance of a rarity being found", and ideally be normalised by observation effort.
- "Where will the next rarity appear" is not what any product attempts; what the data support is "which rarities are plausible this week, and in which habitat/region", which is also what the BTO blog delivers manually.

### Gaps
- No eBird "rare bird forecast" product was found; eBird's Rare Bird Alerts (reports, not forecasts) were not reviewed.
- Full texts of Brooks 2024 (Springer login redirect), the BirdGuides prediction article (403) and the Wiley page for the Ross's Gull paper (403) could not be fetched; facts come from PMC/DOAJ search summaries.
- No Dutch Birding or Dutch forum rarity-forecast write-ups were found; searches in Dutch ("verwachting zeldzame vogels", "vogeltrekverwachting") returned only anecdotes.
- No source quantifies how well human weather-chart predictions perform.

## Synthesis: what a Netherlands rare bird heatmap could copy

### Takeaway
Three design patterns are documented well enough to copy: (1) the crane radar's live ingestion loop and grid-probability rendering (R Shiny, observations API plus KNMI wind, 1 to 5 minute refresh, plain-language four-class legend, location alert); (2) the BirdCast/UvA pattern of training on years of history against reanalysis weather and then driving the model with numerical weather forecasts; (3) the Ross's Gull pattern of species-specific daily occurrence probabilities from synoptic weather classes and local wind. The data that are actually open for a hobby project are KNMI weather and bird-radar products (CC BY 4.0) and the aloft radar archive; eBird S&T and EBP data are not usable in a public website without permission, so the project's own waarneming.nl-derived history is the realistic training base.

### Cited Findings
- Crane radar stack and loop: R Shiny, API tokens at start-up, reactiveTimer loop, one-minute update, four-hour horizon per observation, KNMI hourly forecasts refreshed four times a day, raster probability map, location-based text alert. [De Koning 2025](https://edepot.wur.nl/683479)
- Crane radar cost and effort: about a year and roughly 15,000 to 20,000 euro; 100,000 daily visits at peak. [Nature 646:763-765](https://media.nature.com/original/magazine-assets/d41586-025-03314-y/d41586-025-03314-y.pdf)
- BirdCast forecast recipe: trained on 23 years of radar detections and NARR reanalysis; 6-hourly updates; 24 to 72 h skill 68 to 72 percent. [BirdCast Forecast Maps](https://birdcast.org/migration-tools/migration-forecast-maps/); [bioRxiv 293092](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/293092v1)
- Open Dutch inputs: KNMI FlySafe bird-density composite, CC BY 4.0, continually updated; 5-minute real-time density via Open Data API and WMS. [KNMI Data Platform dataset](https://dataplatform.knmi.nl/dataset/radar-flysafe-nl-composite-1-0); [KNMI Data Platform, tag Birds](https://dataplatform.knmi.nl/dataset/?tags=Birds)
- Closed inputs: eBird S&T data products may not be used in websites or apps without written consent; EBP data only on request to the EBP coordinator. [eBird S&T Terms of Use](https://ebird.org/about/products-access-terms-of-use); [EBP help page](https://eurobirdportal.org/ebp/en/help)
- Rarity forecast blueprint: SOM on pressure patterns plus regression on local wind and season, driven by operational NWP, 1 to 5 day daily probabilities, validated over two seasons. [PMC11199128](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11199128/)
- Observer-coverage caveat for vagrant data. [UEA research portal](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/vagrancy-theories-are-autumn-vagrants-really-reverse-migrants/)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable test-site feature, in increasing difficulty: (a) a climatological weekly "rarity chance" grid per species group from the scraped waarneming.nl history (EBP/eBird-style visual, own data, no licence issue beyond waarneming.nl terms); (b) the same grid conditioned on wind-direction class and synoptic pattern from ERA5 history, driven by the KNMI or ECMWF forecast (Ross's Gull pattern); (c) a live "now" layer using the KNMI 5-minute bird density product as a migration-intensity indicator for drift-migrant days.
- Observation effort normalisation is the main modelling risk; without it the heatmap will reproduce Texel and the coast every week.
- A negotiated Observation International API key would remove the scraping fragility; the crane radar precedent is the strongest argument to use in such a request.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the predictive skill of any rarity-occurrence model for NW Europe; the feasibility of pattern (b) for Dutch rarities is untested in the literature found.
- waarneming.nl's terms for reuse of scraped or API data in a public derived product were not found and should be checked before publishing any heatmap.
