# Environmental data sources for a rare-bird occurrence forecast (NL and neighbours)

Scope: wind and weather (forecast and historical), land cover and habitat, weather-radar bird migration, other candidate predictors, and practical notes for a hobby developer running a small Docker container (about 1 GB RAM) on a VPS, with offline precomputation allowed.

Method and provenance: all documentation pages below were read on 2026-10-24 unless noted. Where I could list a public bucket or directory myself (Aloft S3 bucket, ESA WorldCover S3 bucket, Copernicus DEM S3 bucket, DWD open data directory, ECMWF open data portal, Geofabrik, JRC Global Surface Water on Google Cloud Storage), the numbers are marked "verified by listing" and come from my own HTTP requests on 2026-10-24. Items marked "inference" are my reasoning, not something a page stated. Pages that could not be fetched are listed in the Gaps of each section.

## Key question 1: Wind and weather (forecast at least 3 days ahead, history at least 2 years back, gridded wind for the North Sea, Scandinavia, the Siberia-to-NL corridor and the Atlantic approaches)

### Takeaway
For a near-zero-cost prototype, Open-Meteo covers both needs from one API family: the Forecast API gives up to 16 days ahead with 10 m, 80 m, 120 m, 180 m and pressure-level winds from a blend of ECMWF IFS, DWD ICON and KNMI HARMONIE, and the Historical Weather (ERA5) and Historical Forecast APIs give multi-year history, all under CC BY 4.0 with a free non-commercial tier of 10,000 calls per day. If gridded fields are needed over the whole corridor (Siberia to NL, Scandinavia, Atlantic), ECMWF open data (0.25 degree, global, 15 days, CC BY 4.0) and DWD ICON-EU (about 7 km, 23.5 W to 45 E, 120 h, CC BY 4.0) are the free raw-grid options, while KNMI HARMONIE on the KNMI Data Platform is the best free high-resolution source for the NL domain (CC BY 4.0, GRIB, but only 72 hours retained for the Europe dataset).

Open-Meteo Forecast API
- Hourly wind speed and direction are offered at 10 m, 80 m, 120 m and 180 m plus 10 m gusts; the forecast docs page does not list 100 m wind. Pressure-level variables (temperature, dewpoint, RH, cloud cover, wind speed and direction, geopotential height) are available from 1000 hPa to 30 hPa. Other groups include visibility, precipitation, cloud cover by layer, CAPE, soil variables. [Open-Meteo Forecast API docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)
- Default forecast 7 days, maximum 16 days (forecast_days 0 to 16); past_days accepts 0 to 92. Base URL https://api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast; output JSON, CSV or XLSX. [Open-Meteo Forecast API docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)
- Model table on the docs page: ICON (DWD) 2 to 11 km, 7.5 days, every 3 h; IFS and AIFS (ECMWF) 9 to 25 km, 15 days, every 6 h; UKMO 2 to 10 km, 7 days, hourly; ARPEGE and AROME 1 to 25 km, 4 days, hourly; MET Nordic 1 km, 2.5 days, hourly; KNMI HARMONIE 2 km, 2.5 days, hourly updates; DMI HARMONIE 2 km, 2.5 days, every 3 h; ICON CH 1 to 2 km, 5 days. "Best match" combines a global backbone (ICON, GFS, IFS) with regional models, highest resolution taking precedence. [Open-Meteo Forecast API docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)
- Page shows copyright 2022 to 2026 and example dates of 2026-10-23, so it was current when read. [Open-Meteo Forecast API docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs)

Open-Meteo Historical Weather API (reanalysis)
- Data sources table: ECMWF IFS 9 km hourly, 2017 to present, updated every 6 h with no delay; ERA5 0.25 deg (about 25 km) hourly, 1940 to present, daily with 5-day delay; ERA5-Land 0.1 deg (about 11 km) hourly, 1950 to present, 5-day delay; ERA5-Ensemble 0.5 deg 3-hourly; CERRA 5 km hourly, Europe, 1985 to June 2021; IFS Assimilation Long-Window 9 km 6-hourly, 2024 to present, 2-day delay. [Open-Meteo Historical Weather API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)
- Hourly wind speed and direction at 10 m and 100 m plus gusts; ERA5-Land takes wind from ERA5. Pressure levels are not offered here (only surface and sea-level pressure). Endpoint https://archive-api.open-meteo.com/v1/archive. No maximum date range per request is stated. [Open-Meteo Historical Weather API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)

Open-Meteo Historical Forecast API and Previous Runs API
- Archives the high-resolution forecast models with the same variables as the forecast API, stitched into a continuous hourly series. "Available since" table includes: DWD ICON-EU 0.0625 deg since 2022-11-24; ICON-D2 0.02 deg since 2022-11-24; ECMWF IFS HRES 9 km since 2017-01-01; ECMWF IFS 0.25 deg since 2024-02-03; KNMI HARMONIE Netherlands 2 km and Europe 5.5 km since 2024-07-01; DMI HARMONIE 2 km since 2024-07-01; UK Met Office Global and UKV since 2022-03-01; MET Norway Nordic 1 km since 2022-11-15. [Open-Meteo Historical Forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-forecast-api)

The Previous Runs API gives each variable at fixed lead times of 1 to 7 days, data from January 2024 (GFS from March 2021). [Open-Meteo Historical Forecast API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-forecast-api)

### Inferences
- Corridor coverage: ERA5 (via Open-Meteo archive or CDS) is global, so Siberia-to-NL, Scandinavian and Atlantic history for 2 or more years back is covered at 0.25 deg. For forecasts, ECMWF open data is global at 0.25 deg; ICON-EU open data stops at 45 E, which reaches western Russia but not Siberia proper (Urals are near 60 E), so Siberian tailwind set-up 3 to 5 days ahead needs ECMWF open data or ICON global, or Open-Meteo point queries (which use IFS/GFS/ICON global as backbone).
- Call budget: Open-Meteo returns one location per call in the documented form. At 10,000 calls/day the free tier supports roughly a 5 km national grid (about 1,700 cells for the Netherlands land and near-shore area) refreshed once or twice a day, or a 1 km grid only if cells are subsampled or cached; a per-species habitat heatmap at 1 km can still be driven by wind interpolated from a coarser 5 to 10 km weather grid because wind fields vary smoothly. I did not verify whether Open-Meteo accepts multiple coordinates per call; the docs page as summarised did not mention it, so budget on one location per call until checked.
- Memory: ERA5 and IFS 0.25 deg subsets for a 20 by 30 degree window are a few MB per time step; ICON-EU single-parameter step files are about 1.2 MB compressed. These fit a 1 GB container when processed one parameter and step at a time with cfgrib or eccodes. Pulling whole ECMWF step files (about 95 MB each, 8 GB per run) is feasible on disk but wasteful; use the ecmwf-opendata client's parameter and level filters, or the Open-Meteo point API instead.
- Licensing for Vogelradar: if vogel-radar.nl is a free hobby app with no revenue, it fits Open-Meteo's non-commercial tier; any monetisation (ads, paid tiers) would require the Standard plan (which excludes the historical API) or Professional, or self-hosting the AGPL server. KNMI, ECMWF and DWD data are CC BY 4.0 and carry no such restriction beyond attribution.
- KNMI HARMONIE on KDP is the most direct route to the operational 2 km NL model, but the Europe dataset is deleted after 72 hours and the NL archive only starts January 2026, so for history beyond that, Open-Meteo's Historical Forecast API (KNMI HARMONIE since 2024-07-01) is the practical substitute.
- Met Office DataHub's 1 GB/month free tier is enough for a few UKV point samples but not for routine gridded pulls; treat as optional for Atlantic approaches, since ECMWF and ICON already cover that area.

## Key question 2: Land cover and habitat (freely downloadable for NL plus a buffer into BE and DE, format and size)

### Takeaway
ESA WorldCover 2021 (10 m, 11 classes, CC BY 4.0, public S3 bucket, about 55 MB per 3 by 3 degree COG tile, four tiles cover NL plus the BE/DE buffer) and CORINE 2018 (100 m, 44 classes, free and open under the CLMS data policy) are the practical cross-border layers; LGN2024 (5 m, 51 classes, CC BY 4.0) is the best NL-only thematic layer, and Natura 2000 (public domain via PDOK/RVO), OSM extracts (ODbL, NL 1.3 GB, BE 665 MB, DE 4.5 GB) and JRC Global Surface Water (30 m, free) add protected areas, detailed water and wetland structure.

### Inferences
- Tile selection: the Netherlands plus a buffer into Belgium and northwest Germany (roughly 2 to 9 E, 50 to 54.5 N) falls in the four ESA WorldCover tiles N51E003, N51E006, N54E003 and N54E006 (and N48E003/N48E006 if the buffer reaches the Ardennes and Eifel), so about 220 to 330 MB of COGs; at 10 m this is still large to keep resident, so precompute per-class fractions on a 1 km or 500 m grid offline and store a few MB of aggregated rasters.
- CORINE at 100 m with 44 classes is the easiest "habitat class" layer to map onto per-species preferences (for example, wetlands, salt marsh, heath, broad-leaved forest), and the same legend applies across NL, BE and DE, which matters for a cross-border buffer; WorldCover's 11 classes are too coarse thematically for rarities tied to specific habitats but add fine spatial detail.
- LGN2024 is NL-only and the 5 m national raster will be several GB uncompressed (about 300 km by 300 km at 25 square metres per cell is about 3.6 billion cells; inference, size not published on the pages read); aggregate it offline to class fractions per 1 km cell and never load it in the VPS container.
- For NNN, use provincial public-domain files where needed rather than the national INSPIRE file with unknown licence.
- OSM is the only free source for coastline, dune, mudflat, reedbed and "sewage works" style micro-habitats with a single consistent schema across NL, BE and DE; process the PBF offline with osmium or pyosmium and keep only derived rasters or GeoPackages.

## Key question 3: Weather-radar bird migration detection (KNMI radars, vol2bird and bioRad, ENRAM, GloBAM, FlySafe, Aloft; near-real-time availability; forecasts; Trektellen as ground proxy)

### Takeaway
The Aloft bucket (CC0, public S3, no key) already provides vol2bird vertical profiles for the two Dutch radars (Herwijnen nlhrw, Den Helder nldhl) plus Belgian, German and Danish radars, with 5-minute HDF5 profiles appearing within a few hours to a day and daily VPTS CSVs about one to two days after the night in question (verified by listing), which is enough for "last nights" migration intensity but not for same-night nowcasting. No public European migration forecast product exists as of October 2026: the FlySafe forecasts for the Dutch Air Force are operated by KNMI with UvA models, and a Europe-wide forecast model is described as still in development; Trektellen counts are visible in near real time but are explicitly not open data and need permission for any reuse.

### Inferences
- Practical pipeline: a nightly job at about 05:00 UTC can fetch the previous two daily VPTS CSVs for nlhrw, nldhl and the nearest BE and DE radars (bejab, behel, deess, deemd when active, dehnr) and compute a nightly migration traffic rate; because daily files sometimes arrive two days late (the 19 October file came on 22 October), the job should also pull the 5-minute HDF5 files for the most recent night, which were landing 4 to 18 hours after observation in the listings I made. Each HDF5 is about 25 KB, so a full night for five radars is under 10 MB, fine for a 1 GB container.
- Integration can be done in Python (h5py, pandas) following the VPTS CSV schema rather than running R in the container; bioRad is better used offline for validation. Running vol2bird on raw KNMI volumes is possible with the KDP radar datasets but adds heavy dependencies and is unnecessary while Aloft is current.
- Migration intensity from radar is a proxy for nocturnal passerine migration volume, not for rarities directly; its value for a rare-bird heatmap is as a "fallout" or "influx" indicator when combined with wind shifts and precipitation, which is what the UvA models for FlySafe exploit. A home-grown forecast could regress Aloft nightly MTR on Open-Meteo wind and precipitation (2017 onward history for nlhrw), which is cheap offline.
- Trektellen cannot be scraped or integrated without written permission; a request to the administrator (and Sovon as partner) would be needed, and it should be treated as a validation dataset rather than a live feed.

### Inferences
- Tides matter mainly for coastal waders and seabirds (roost timing, mudflat exposure); use the astronomical tide series from WaterWebservices for a handful of stations (for example Den Helder, Harlingen, Vlissingen, Hoek van Holland) and precompute "hours around high water" per coastal cell. Because historical forecasts are archived, pull forecasts once per 6 h cycle.
- Anomalies: compute a 1991 to 2020 (or 2010 to 2025) climatology per 0.25 deg ERA5 cell offline once (one call per cell per year range from the archive API), then daily anomalies need only the latest week; frost-day counts and "days below 0 C in the last 7 days" for winter influxes are simple derivatives.
- NDVI is the weakest predictor-to-cost ratio for rare birds: Sentinel-2 processing on CDSE is feasible within the free quota for a few dates a month at 10 m but adds complexity; MODIS 1 km 16-day NDVI from Earthdata (free account) is simpler and matches a 1 km heatmap grid. Only pursue if a species group (for example geese or larks on arable land) needs greenness.
- Elevation and distance-to-coast are static; precompute once with Copernicus DEM (two to four tiles, about 100 MB) and OSM coastline, store as small 1 km rasters. AHN at 0.5 m is far more than needed and its national size is impractical for the VPS.
- Light pollution is static and mainly useful as a proxy for urbanisation or for nocturnal flight-call predictors; one VNL GeoTIFF clipped to NL is a few MB.

## Key question 5: Practical notes (coordinate systems, tooling, sensible grid resolution for a national heatmap on a 1 GB VPS)

### Takeaway
Everything Dutch (PDOK, LGN, AHN, Natura 2000) is in RD New (EPSG:28992), European Copernicus rasters are in EPSG:3035, global rasters (WorldCover, DEM, GSW, VIIRS) are in EPSG:4326, and all weather APIs are WGS84 points, so pick one analysis grid (EPSG:28992 at 1 km is the natural choice for a national heatmap; 2 to 5 km for weather fields) and reproject every static layer to it once, offline. The VPS container should only ever hold the precomputed static rasters (a few MB each) plus the latest weather and radar deltas.

### Cited Findings
- Dutch sources explicitly tied to RD New or PDOK: Natura 2000 via PDOK OGC API Features, WMS, WFS and ATOM [PDOK Natura 2000](https://www.pdok.nl/introductie/-/article/natura-2000); AHN via WMS, WCS and ATOM map sheets [PDOK AHN](https://www.pdok.nl/introductie/-/article/actueel-hoogtebestand-nederland-ahn); LGN latest version on PDOK as WMS only [PDOK LGN](https://www.pdok.nl/introductie/-/article/landelijk-grondgebruik-nederland-lgn-). (The PDOK pages read did not print the EPSG code; RD New EPSG:28992 as the PDOK default is my prior knowledge, flagged as not verified in this session.)
- European Copernicus land products are delivered in EPSG:3035 (CLC2018, CLC+ Backbone 2021, HRL Imperviousness 2021). [CLC2018 dataset page](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/corine-land-cover/clc2018); [CLC+ Backbone 2021](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/clc-backbone/clc-backbone-2021); [HRL Imperviousness Density 2021](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/high-resolution-layer-imperviousness/imperviousness-density-2021)

Sources as cited in the sections above.
- Rate-limit anchors: Open-Meteo 10,000 calls/day free [Open-Meteo pricing](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing); KNMI registered key 1000 requests/hour [KNMI Open Data API docs](https://developer.dataplatform.knmi.nl/open-data-api); Rijkswaterstaat 160,000 observations per request [Rijkswaterstaat waterdata](https://rijkswaterstaatdata.nl/waterdata/); CDSE 4 concurrent downloads and 10,000 openEO credits/month [CDSE quotas](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/Quotas.html).

### Inferences
- Grid: a 1 km EPSG:28992 grid over the Netherlands is roughly 300 by 350 cells (about 100,000 cells including sea buffer), which as float32 is under 1 MB per layer; even 30 static habitat layers plus a few dynamic layers stay well under 100 MB in RAM. A 5 km grid (about 4,000 cells) is adequate for wind and pressure fields and keeps Open-Meteo usage within the free tier at one call per cell per day, with bilinear upsampling to 1 km at render time.
- Tooling (standard choices, not verified against documentation in this session): rasterio and GDAL for reprojection and COG reading over HTTP (range requests on the WorldCover and DEM COGs avoid downloading whole tiles), xarray with cfgrib or eccodes for GRIB from ECMWF, DWD and KNMI, h5py or pandas for Aloft HDF5 and VPTS CSV, pyproj for EPSG:28992 to 4326 conversions, osmium or pyosmium for OSM PBF filtering. Run all of this offline on a laptop or a one-off larger machine; ship only NumPy arrays or small GeoTIFFs to the VPS.
- Separation of concerns for the 1 GB container: a scheduled job (cron or similar) that fetches JSON from Open-Meteo and CSV from Aloft and Rijkswaterstaat, writes a small per-cell feature table, and a stateless renderer that multiplies species habitat weights by weather and migration factors. Avoid loading GDAL into the production container at all if the static layers are pre-rasterised to the final grid.
- Memory hazards: never open the national LGN 5 m raster, the full AHN tiles or the Geofabrik PBF on the VPS; never pull whole ECMWF step files (95 MB each) when a handful of parameters over a 20 by 30 degree window are needed; prefer the ecmwf-opendata client's filtering or Open-Meteo point queries.
